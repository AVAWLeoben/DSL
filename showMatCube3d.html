How to read the stack

Each translucent plane represents one spectral sheet from the data cube. Together, the 220 planes form a tangible 3D stack. You can see through the cube and understand that hyperspectral data is collected as many neighboring slices rather than one ordinary image.

The displayed geometry preserves the idea of a real cuboid but exaggerates visibility slightly so the individual sheets remain distinguishable in the browser.

MATLAB source

cube = imnData / max(imnData,[],'all');
v = volshow(cube);

Instead of real-time volume rendering, the browser shows 220 pre-rendered slices as CSS 3D planes. This is lighter, clearer for teaching, and works on ordinary student laptops.
